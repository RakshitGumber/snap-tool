# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Single Filter is for solo creators, developers, indie builders, and small maker-led teams who need polished social graphics without opening a full design suite. They are usually moving quickly: sharing a launch, highlighting a GitHub project, promoting a YouTube video, or turning a useful link into a post-ready image.

Their job is to paste a link, get credible visual options, make light adjustments, and export a clean PNG for the platform they are posting to.

## Product Purpose

Single Filter turns YouTube, GitHub, and website links into exportable social post graphics. It combines link metadata, platform canvas presets, template variants, background choices, and a focused editor so users can produce a professional-looking post in a few steps.

Success means users reach a publishable export quickly, with enough control to make the result feel intentional but not enough friction to break momentum. Snap Tool is the repository and export-file alias for the same product.

## Brand Personality

Crisp, capable, and quiet. The product should feel fast, precise, and creator-focused without becoming sterile, enterprise-heavy, or precious.

Copy should be direct and useful. The interface should trust the user, avoid over-explaining, and reserve emphasis for the next action.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS gloss: vague productivity copy, oversized marketing sections, glassy decoration, gradient-heavy polish, and template-like AI aesthetics.

Do not drift into a pro design suite. Single Filter should not feel like Figma, Photoshop, or a campaign management platform with too many manual controls.

Do not become a casual toy. Motion, templates, and color should support credible exports, not gimmicks.

## Design Principles

1. Editor first. The creation surface is the product; marketing exists to point users into the editor.
2. Strong defaults over endless controls. Templates, platform sizes, and backgrounds should make the first result credible before customization.
3. Preserve creator momentum. Common actions should be obvious, fast, and reversible.
4. Earn trust through familiar affordances. Buttons, panels, inputs, export controls, undo, redo, and selection should behave like reliable product UI.
5. Let the canvas carry the expression. The surrounding interface should stay restrained so the exported graphic can be the colorful part.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as the baseline. Maintain clear contrast, semantic controls, keyboard access, visible focus states, meaningful labels, and reduced-motion respect.

Interactive controls must remain usable with a keyboard and screen reader. Color should never be the only way to understand state, especially for selection, errors, disabled controls, and export feedback.
